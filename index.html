      About this archive

      This site maps every photograph in Box B23: 36 field photographs from 2004, covering villages in Tung Chung Valley, Ma Wan Chung and Sha Lo Wan on Lantau Island. Made for ANTH 3323B Digital Humanities, Module 1, by LUN Kam Wing, Rain.

      How certain each location is

      LevelWhat it meansPhotographs

      A point is an estimate, even when its coordinates look exact. Each record says what its point marks: the estimated camera position, the photographed subject, or only the general area. Many Low points share one village reference point and don’t show where the photographer stood.

      How the map was made

      • Locations came from the archive captions, Google Map and satellite comparison, the HKMS map and the aerial photos. Each record lists its method and evidence.
      • Aerial photos were georeferenced in QGIS with a first-order polynomial to Hong Kong 1980 Grid (EPSG:2326). Point coordinates are stored in WGS 84 (EPSG:4326).
      • Sha Lo Wan: aerial photo CW59283, 27 September 2004, flying height 4,000 ft. 7 control points, mean error 6.43621 pixels.
      • Tung Chung: aerial photo CW55194, 10 February 2004 (printed 10.2.2004), flying height 8,000 ft. 6 control points, mean error 6.88234 pixels.

      Limitations

      • The image file for B23P10 is missing from the working copy of Box B23. Its record stays in the archive, based on the existing notes, but its subject couldn’t be rechecked against the photograph.
      • The aerial photos only cover the areas around the mapped photographs. Elsewhere you see the present-day background.
      • The aerial photos date from February and September 2004, while the field photographs are recorded as June 2004, so they may not show identical conditions.
      • Each aerial photo was aligned with only 6 or 7 control points, so its fit may be weaker away from those points. Use it for comparing landscapes, not for exact measurement.
      • The capture date of the modern imagery isn’t stated in its documentation, so “today” means the current service, not a known date.
      • Maps and photographs show different things. A feature missing from a map may still have existed on the ground.
